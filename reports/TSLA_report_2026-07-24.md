> [!info] Business Overview
Tesla, Inc. is a vertically integrated electric vehicle (EV) manufacturer and clean energy company. The company designs, manufactures, and sells electric vehicles (Model S, 3, X, Y, Cybertruck, Semi), energy generation and storage products (Powerwall, Megapack, Solar Roof), and related services. Automotive revenue constitutes the vast majority of total revenue (~85%+), with energy generation and storage growing but still a smaller contributor. Tesla operates its own direct sales network, supercharger infrastructure, and has a growing autonomous driving software business (FSD). The company's competitive position is under severe pressure: it faces intensifying competition from [[BYD]], [[XPEV]], [[NIO]], and legacy OEMs like [[GM]] and [[FORD]] in EVs, while its once-insurmountable cost and technology lead has eroded. Tesla's brand remains strong, but its product lineup is aging (Model 3/Y launched 2017/2020) and the Cybertruck has been a production and demand disappointment.

> [!example] Financial Analysis
**Revenue**: FY2025 total revenue of $94.8B is essentially flat year-over-year (FY2024: $97.7B; FY2023: $96.8B). This represents a **-3.0% decline** from FY2024, indicating demand saturation and pricing pressure.

**Margins**: Gross profit fell to $17.1B in FY2025 (18.0% margin) from $17.5B in FY2024 (17.9% margin) and $17.7B in FY2023 (18.3% margin). The gross margin is compressing as Tesla cuts prices to maintain volume. Operating income collapsed to $4.4B in FY2025 (4.6% margin) from $7.1B in FY2024 (7.2% margin) and $8.9B in FY2023 (9.2% margin) — a **51% decline in operating profit** over two years.

**Expenses**: SG&A surged to $5.8B (up 13% YoY) and R&D to $6.4B (up 41% YoY), far outpacing revenue growth. Stock-based compensation rose to $2.8B in FY2025 from $2.0B in FY2024.

**Cash Flow**: Operating cash flow was $14.7B in FY2025 (down from $14.9B in FY2024 and $13.3B in FY2023). Capex was $8.5B, up from $11.3B in FY2024. Free cash flow (operating CF - capex) was $6.2B, down from $3.6B in FY2024 and $4.4B in FY2023. The company raised $5.6B in debt in FY2025.

**Balance Sheet**: Total assets of $137.8B vs total liabilities of $54.9B. Cash & equivalents of $16.5B plus short-term investments of $27.5B = $44.0B in cash/securities. Total debt is $6.6B long-term. Net cash position of ~$37.4B is strong, but the cash burn from operations relative to capex is concerning.

**Earnings**: Diluted EPS fell to $1.08 in FY2025 from $2.04 in FY2024 and $4.30 in FY2023 — a **75% decline** from peak.

At the current price of ~$320, TSLA has a market cap of approximately $1.0 trillion (based on ~3.2B basic shares outstanding). Key multiples:
- **P/E (trailing)**: ~85x FY2025 diluted EPS of $1.08 — absurdly high for a company with declining earnings.
- **P/E (FY2024)**: ~45x FY2024 EPS of $2.04.
- **EV/EBITDA**: EV = $1.0T market cap - $37.4B net cash = ~$963B. EBITDA = operating income ($4.4B) + D&A ($5.0B) = $9.4B. EV/EBITDA = **~102x**.
- **P/S**: ~10.5x trailing revenue.

For context, [[NVDA]] trades at ~35x earnings with 100%+ revenue growth. [[AVGO]] trades at ~25x earnings with 40%+ growth. Legacy automakers like [[GM]] trade at 5-6x earnings. Tesla's valuation implies either a massive earnings recovery or a speculative premium for future technologies (FSD, Optimus robot, energy) that have yet to materialize in any meaningful financial contribution. The current multiple is **not justified** by any fundamental metric.

> [!warning] Risks
1. **Demand Collapse & Price War** `#critical`
   Tesla's revenue is flat despite aggressive price cuts. The EV market is oversupplied, and competitors like [[BYD]] are undercutting Tesla on cost. Further price cuts would destroy margins and earnings.
2. **Margin Erosion** `#critical`
   Operating margin has halved from 9.2% to 4.6% in two years. With R&D and SG&A growing faster than revenue, margins could turn negative if revenue declines further.
3. **Valuation Re-Rating** `#critical`
   At 85x declining earnings, any negative catalyst (missed delivery numbers, margin miss, macro slowdown) could trigger a violent multiple compression. The recent 21.6% drop in 10 days may be the start.
4. **Elon Musk Distraction & Brand Risk** `#medium`
   Insider trading filings show Musk exercising options at $0-$23 and selling at $402-$450. His political controversies and attention on X/Twitter, xAI, and other ventures create governance and brand perception risks.
5. **Cybertruck & New Product Execution** `#medium`
   The Cybertruck has been a production nightmare and demand disappointment. The "next-gen" vehicle platform remains unproven. Capital spending of $8.5B in FY2025 suggests heavy investment with uncertain returns.
6. **Autonomous Driving & Regulatory Risk** `#medium`
   FSD revenue recognition remains lumpy. Regulatory approval for unsupervised FSD is years away in most jurisdictions. The "robotaxi" narrative is speculative and not reflected in current financials.

> [!tip] Catalysts
1. **Q2 2026 Delivery Beat** `#near-term`
   If Tesla reports better-than-feared deliveries in the upcoming quarter, the stock could bounce from oversold levels. However, the trend is clearly negative.
2. **FSD v13 / Robotaxi Announcement** `#medium-term`
   Any positive regulatory news or a commercial robotaxi launch in a major market (e.g., California, Texas) could reignite the narrative. However, monetization remains years away.
3. **Energy Storage Acceleration** `#medium-term`
   Megapack and Powerwall revenue is growing. If energy becomes a material profit contributor (10%+ of gross profit), it could provide a floor for valuation.
4. **Cost Cutting / Restructuring** `#near-term`
   If Tesla announces meaningful headcount reductions or capex cuts to protect margins, the market may react positively. The $390M restructuring charge in FY2025 suggests some action is being taken.
