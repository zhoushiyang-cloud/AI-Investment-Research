> [!info] Business Overview
> Tesla, Inc. operates two primary segments:
> 1.  **Automotive** (estimated ~85% of revenue): Design, manufacture, and sale of electric vehicles (Model S, 3, X, Y, Cybertruck). Revenue also includes automotive regulatory credits, non-warranty service, used vehicle sales, and insurance. The company sells directly to consumers and operates its own service centers and Supercharger network.
> 2.  **Energy Generation & Storage** (estimated ~15% of revenue): Development, manufacturing, and installation of solar energy systems (Solar Roof, Solar Panels) and energy storage products (Powerwall, Megapack) for residential, commercial, and utility customers.
> 
> **Competitive Position**: TSLA is the global leader in BEV production by volume, but its market share is eroding rapidly in key markets (China, EU) due to intense competition from [[BYD]], [[XPeng]], and legacy OEMs like [[GM]] and [[VW]]. The company's competitive moat now rests less on its auto business and more on its software (FSD), AI infrastructure (Dojo), and energy storage scale.

> [!example] Financial Analysis
> - **Revenue Trends**: FY2025 revenue of $94.8B declined from $97.7B in FY2024 and $96.8B in FY2023. This marks the first annual revenue decline in recent history, driven by lower average selling prices (ASP) and delivery volume headwinds.
> - **Margins**: Gross profit fell to $17.1B (18.0% margin) vs $17.5B (17.9% margin) in FY2024. Operating income plunged to $4.4B (4.6% margin) from $7.1B (7.2% margin) in FY2024. The primary culprit was a 41% surge in R&D expenses to $6.4B (from $4.5B in FY2024) and a 13% increase in SG&A to $5.8B.
> - **Cash Flow**: Operating cash flow was strong at $14.7B in FY2025, roughly flat vs $14.9B in FY2024. However, free cash flow (FCF) was significantly impacted by $8.5B in CapEx and $37.1B in investment purchases, resulting in negative FCF for the year.
> - **Balance Sheet**: Extremely healthy. Total assets of $137.8B. The company has $44.1B in cash and short-term investments against $8.4B in total debt (including $6.7B in long-term debt). Total shareholders' equity stands at $82.1B.

> [!info] Valuation Assessment
> - **Current Multiples**: At a $380.84 share price and $1.43T market cap, TSLA trades at a trailing P/E of ~377x (based on FY2025 diluted EPS of $1.08) and an EV/EBITDA of ~122x (EV = $1.43T - $8.1B net cash = $1.42T; EBITDA = $11.8B).
> - **Peer Comparison**: This is a massive premium to traditional auto peers like [[GM]] (P/E ~7x) and [[TM]] (P/E ~10x). It also trades at a significant premium to high-growth tech peers like [[NVDA]] (P/E ~50x).
> - **Justification**: The valuation is not supported by current financials. It is entirely a bet on future optionality: the robotaxi network (Cybercab), the Optimus humanoid robot, and a dominant position in AI/Dojo. The current auto business is being valued at near-zero or negative value. This is a "show me" story.

> [!warning] Risks
> 1. **Demand Destruction & Margin Erosion** `#critical`
>    Revenue declined 2.9% YoY in FY2025 despite record deliveries. Price cuts are destroying margins (Op Margin fell to 4.6%). If volume cannot offset lower ASPs, the auto business becomes a cash drain.
> 2. **Elon Musk Key-Person & Brand Risk** `#critical`
>    CEO Elon Musk is synonymous with TSLA. His increasing focus on DOGE, X, and AI ventures, combined with polarizing political activity, is alienating the core customer base (liberal, environmentally-conscious) and distracting management.
> 3. **Robotaxi / FSD Regulatory & Tech Risk** `#medium`
>    The entire bull thesis hinges on unsupervised FSD and a robotaxi network. Any delay in regulatory approval (especially in California/Texas) or a major safety incident would crater the stock. The technology is still Level 2 driver-assist, not Level 4/5 autonomy.
> 4. **Cybertruck Hype Fading** `#medium`
>    The Cybertruck launch has been plagued by production issues and reports of quality problems. It is unlikely to be a high-volume, high-margin vehicle, and its polarizing design limits its addressable market.

> [!tip] Catalysts
> 1. **Robotaxi Network Launch** `#near-term`
>    A commercial launch of the Cybercab in a major US market (e.g., Austin or LA) by H2 2026 would be the single biggest catalyst, validating the autonomous thesis and unlocking a new recurring revenue stream.
> 2. **Energy Storage Breakout** `#near-term`
>    Megapack and Powerwall margins are structurally higher than auto. Continued exponential growth in this segment (driven by grid-scale storage demand) could offset auto weakness and re-rate the stock as an energy company.
> 3. **FSD v13 / v14 Supervised Milestone** `#medium-term`
>    A demonstrable leap in FSD capability (e.g., achieving "safety parity" with human drivers by a factor of 10x) would de-risk the regulatory path and build investor confidence in the technology's value.

The core automotive business is facing declining revenue, collapsing margins, and rising competition. While the long-term vision (robotaxi, AI, energy) is compelling, the execution risk is immense and the timeline is uncertain. At 377x trailing earnings, the stock has no margin of safety.
