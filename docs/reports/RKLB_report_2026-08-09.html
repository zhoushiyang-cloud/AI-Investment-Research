ℹ️ Business Overview

Rocket Lab USA, Inc. is a vertically integrated space company headquartered in Long Beach, CA, with 2,600 employees. The company operates two primary segments:

  1. Launch Services (~40% of revenue): The Electron small-lift vehicle is the workhorse, with a proven track record of commercial and government launches. The company is actively developing Neutron, a medium-lift vehicle (8-ton payload) targeting the constellation deployment market, with first launch expected in 2025-2026.

  2. Space Systems (~60% of revenue): This includes the Photon satellite bus, spacecraft components, and on-orbit management services. The acquisition of SolAero (solar panels) and Sinclair (reaction wheels) has expanded the component portfolio. The company also provides full constellation management, a differentiator versus pure-play launchers.

Competitive Position: RKLB is the #2 US launch provider by cadence (behind SpaceX), but holds a unique position as the only company offering both launch and satellite manufacturing under one roof. Key customers span commercial constellations (e.g., Kuiper-adjacent programs), defense (US Space Force, NRO), and NASA. The company's vertical integration—from components to launch to operations—creates a sticky, high-margin ecosystem that pure-play peers (e.g., ASTS, SPCE) lack.

Key Strategic Focus: The Neutron program is the critical growth catalyst. If successful, it opens the medium-lift market (currently dominated by SpaceX Falcon 9) and enables RKLB to bid on larger defense and commercial contracts. Management has guided to Neutron's first launch in 2025, with a focus on reusability to drive down cost-per-launch.

📊 Financial Analysis

Revenue Growth: FY2025 revenue of $601.8M represents +38% YoY growth (from $436.2M in FY2024), accelerating from +78% growth in FY2024 (from $244.6M in FY2023). This demonstrates strong demand across both launch and space systems.

Margins: Gross profit improved to $207.2M (34.4% margin) in FY2025, up from $116.1M (26.6%) in FY2024. This expansion reflects higher launch cadence, improved production efficiency, and a richer mix of higher-margin space systems revenue. However, operating losses widened to -$228.8M (from -$189.8M) due to heavy R&D investment of $270.7M (+55% YoY) into Neutron and production scale-up.

Cash Flow & Balance Sheet: Operating cash flow was -$165.5M in FY2025 (improved from -$48.9M in FY2024, though the FY2024 figure was flattered by working capital timing). The company invested $156.3M in capex (primarily Neutron infrastructure) and $132.4M in acquisitions. Critically, the company raised $1.07B in equity during FY2025, ending the year with $828.7M cash and $187.9M in short-term investments—a total liquidity position of $1.02B. Total debt is minimal at $1.7M (current portion) plus $44.0M long-term debt, leaving the balance sheet under-levered.

Key Ratios: The company is burning cash at ~$165M/year operationally, but with $1.02B in liquidity, it has a ~6-year cash runway at current burn rates—ample time to reach profitability or raise additional capital on favorable terms. The beta of 2.55 reflects high volatility and growth-stage risk.
