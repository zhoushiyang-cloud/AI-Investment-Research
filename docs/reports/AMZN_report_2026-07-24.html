ℹ️ Business Overview

  • Amazon.com, Inc. operates through three primary segments: North America e-commerce, International e-commerce, and Amazon Web Services (AWS).

  • AWS is the dominant cloud infrastructure provider, competing with MSFT (Azure) and GOOGL (GCP). It is the primary profit engine, generating the vast majority of operating income.

  • E-commerce (North America + International) remains the largest revenue contributor but operates on thin margins. The company is investing heavily in same-day delivery and logistics to defend market share against WMT and TGT.

  • Key growth drivers: AWS AI services (Bedrock, Trainium), advertising (high-margin, double-digit growth), and third-party seller services (FBA).

  • Revenue breakdown (FY2025): Total revenue of $716.9B. While segment-level data is not provided, historical trends suggest AWS accounts for ~15-17% of revenue but >60% of operating profit.

📊 Financial Analysis

  • Revenue: Grew from $574.8B (FY2023) → $638.0B (FY2024) → $716.9B (FY2025), a 12.4% YoY increase. This represents a clear acceleration from the 11.0% growth in FY2024.

  • Margins: Gross margin improved to 21.4% in FY2025 (from 20.7% in FY2024). Operating margin expanded to 11.2% (from 10.8% in FY2024), driven by cost efficiencies and higher-margin AWS/AI mix.

  • Profitability: Net income surged to $77.7B in FY2025 (EPS diluted: $7.17), up from $59.2B ($5.53) in FY2024 and $30.4B ($2.90) in FY2023. This represents a 31% YoY net income growth.

  • Cash Flow: Operating cash flow was $139.5B in FY2025, up from $115.9B in FY2024. However, capex (PP&E purchases) jumped to $131.8B (from $83.0B), reflecting aggressive AI infrastructure spending. Free cash flow (OCF - capex) was ~$7.7B, down from ~$32.9B in FY2024.

  • Balance Sheet: Total assets of $818.0B vs. total liabilities of $415.9B. Cash & equivalents + short-term investments = $123.0B, against long-term debt of $65.6B. Net cash position is strong at ~$57.4B, providing ample liquidity for investment and buybacks.

  • Trailing P/E: $233.66 / $7.17 diluted EPS = ~32.6x. This is a premium to the S&P 500 (~22x) but in line with Amazon’s 5-year average forward P/E of ~35x.

  • Justification: The premium is justified by AWS’s dominant market position, the high-margin advertising business, and the optionality from AI investments. The stock is not cheap, but it is fairly valued relative to its growth trajectory.

    2. AWS faces intensifying competition from MSFT Azure and GOOGL GCP. If AI workloads shift to competitors or if AWS’s AI chip (Trainium) fails to gain traction, revenue growth could slow, compressing the multiple.

  2. Capex Overshoot / ROI Uncertainty #critical

    3. FY2025 capex surged to $131.8B (up 59% YoY). If the massive investment in AI infrastructure does not yield proportional revenue/profit growth, returns on capital will decline, leading to margin pressure and potential write-downs.

  3. Regulatory & Antitrust Risk #medium

    4. The FTC lawsuit and potential EU Digital Markets Act actions could force changes to Amazon’s marketplace practices, impacting third-party seller fees and advertising revenue.

  4. Insider Selling Signal #medium

    5. Recent insider sales at prices between $239 and $266 (above current $233.66) suggest management may view the stock as fully valued near-term, though this could be routine portfolio diversification.

  5. Macroeconomic Slowdown #medium

    6. A recession would pressure consumer discretionary spending (e-commerce) and enterprise cloud budgets (AWS), potentially slowing revenue growth below 10%.

💡 Catalysts

  1. AWS AI Revenue Acceleration #near-term

    2. As AI workloads migrate to the cloud, AWS’s Bedrock and SageMaker services should see accelerating revenue. Q1/Q2 FY2026 earnings could show AWS growth re-accelerating to 20%+.

  2. Advertising Margin Expansion #near-term

    3. Amazon’s advertising business (estimated >$50B revenue) is high-margin and growing faster than e-commerce. Continued share gains from META and GOOGL could drive operating margin above 12%.

  3. Free Cash Flow Inflection #medium-term

    4. As AI capex peaks (likely in FY2026), FCF should rebound sharply. If capex growth slows to 10-15% while OCF continues to grow, FCF could exceed $50B, driving a significant re-rating.

  4. Share Buyback Resumption #medium-term

    5. Amazon has historically been conservative with buybacks. With a strong balance sheet and stock at a reasonable multiple, a new buyback authorization could be a positive signal.
