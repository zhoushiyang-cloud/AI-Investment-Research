ℹ️ Business Overview

Amazon operates three primary segments:

  • North America Retail (~60% of revenue): E-commerce, physical stores, and advertising. The segment has seen margin expansion as regionalization efforts and fulfillment efficiency gains take hold. FY2025 operating income of $79.975B (company-wide) reflects retail profitability at scale.

  • International (~25% of revenue): Global e-commerce operations. Historically a drag, but improving logistics and Prime adoption are narrowing losses.

  • AWS (~15-17% of revenue, ~60-70% of operating profit): The crown jewel. Cloud infrastructure, database, analytics, and AI/ML services. AWS is the primary driver of the company's 12.1% operating margin and is the key beneficiary of enterprise AI spend.

Competitive Position: Amazon holds a dominant #1 position in US e-commerce (~40% share) and is the global leader in cloud infrastructure (alongside MSFT Azure and GOOGL GCP). Its competitive moat is built on:

  • Prime ecosystem (logistics network, video, music) driving customer loyalty and frequency

  • Fulfillment by Amazon (FBA) creating a self-reinforcing flywheel with third-party sellers

  • AWS scale advantages in compute, storage, and AI training/inference

  • Advertising business (high-margin, growing 20%+ annually) that monetizes retail traffic

The company employs 1.576M people and is led by CEO Andy Jassy, who has driven a significant cost discipline and profitability transformation since 2022.

⚠️ Risks

  1. AI Capex Overbuild / Diminishing Returns #critical

    2. Capex of $131.8B (22.3% of revenue) assumes AI demand materializes. If AWS AI services (Bedrock, SageMaker, Trainium) fail to gain traction vs. MSFT/NVDA partnerships, ROIC will suffer. A slowdown in cloud growth (currently ~19% YoY) would expose the negative FCF.

  1. Regulatory / Antitrust Breakup Risk #critical

    2. FTC lawsuit (filed 2023) and EU Digital Markets Act compliance could force structural changes to retail marketplace or AWS. A forced breakup or behavioral remedies could destroy $200-400B in value. Recent insider selling (multiple transactions at $239-266) may signal management concern.

  1. Retail Margin Reversion #medium

    2. Current retail margins benefit from inflation-driven pricing power and regionalization. A consumer recession (Consumer Cyclical sector) could pressure discretionary spend, forcing discounting and margin compression. The 17.4% net margin includes a one-time $16.8B gain — core margin is closer to 11-12%.

  1. Competition Intensification #medium

    2. WMT and TGT are investing heavily in e-commerce and same-day delivery. MSFT and GOOGL are aggressively discounting cloud contracts. SHOP is enabling more off-Amazon DTC commerce. Any share loss in retail or AWS would pressure the growth premium.

  1. Labor & Operational Risk #medium

    2. 1.576M employees creates significant labor cost exposure. Unionization efforts (e.g., Staten Island) and wage inflation could pressure the 12.1% operating margin. Warehouse automation (robotics) is the mitigation, but transition costs are real.

  1. Valuation Multiple Compression #low

    2. At $277.42, the stock is near its 52-week high. If the Fed delays rate cuts or AI sentiment cools broadly, the entire mega-cap tech complex could de-rate 10-15% despite strong fundamentals.

    2. If management signals 2027 capex will be flat or down vs. 2026, FCF will inflect sharply positive. This could trigger a re-rating as the market shifts from "negative FCF" to "FCF machine" narrative. Watch for commentary on the Q3 2026 earnings call.

    2. Amazon Ads is growing 20%+ and carries 50%+ incremental margins. As ad load increases on Prime Video (currently under-monetized) and sponsored products expand, this segment could add 200-300bps to overall operating margin over 12-18 months.

  1. Project Kuiper (Satellite Internet) #medium-term

    2. While early, Kuiper could launch commercial service in 2026-2027, creating a new high-margin recurring revenue stream and competing with TSLA's Starlink. This is optionality not priced into the stock.

  1. Retail International Profitability #medium-term

    2. International segment losses are narrowing. If the segment reaches breakeven (likely in 2026), it adds $2-3B to operating income and validates the global expansion strategy.

  1. Potential S&P 500 / Index Inclusion Weight Increase #low

    2. As AMZN's market cap grows ($2.98T), index funds must buy more shares, providing structural demand. Any announcement of a stock split (to lower share price) would also attract retail inflows.
