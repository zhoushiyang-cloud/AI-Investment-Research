ℹ️ Business Overview

  • Company: Advanced Micro Devices, Inc. (AMD) is a global semiconductor leader headquartered in Santa Clara, CA, with 31,000 employees. It competes directly with NVDA in GPUs and INTC in CPUs.

  • Segments: The company operates in two primary segments:

  1. Computing and Graphics: x86 microprocessors (Ryzen, Threadripper), chipsets, and GPUs (Radeon, Radeon Pro). This segment targets consumer PCs and professional graphics.

  2. Enterprise, Embedded and Semi-Custom: Server processors (EPYC), embedded solutions, and custom SoCs for game consoles (Sony PlayStation, Microsoft Xbox).

  • Competitive Position: AMD is the #2 player in both the x86 CPU market (behind INTC) and the discrete GPU market (behind NVDA). Its key competitive advantage is the "Zen" architecture and chiplet design, which have allowed it to gain significant share in data center CPUs. The company is also a major beneficiary of the AI infrastructure buildout, supplying GPUs (Instinct MI series) for training and inference.

  • Revenue Drivers: The primary growth driver is the Data Center segment (included within both segments), which is fueled by AI. The semi-custom business is cyclical, tied to console lifecycles.

📊 Financial Analysis

  • Revenue Trends: Revenue has grown strongly: $22.68B (FY2023) → $25.79B (FY2024) → $34.64B (FY2025). This represents a 34% YoY increase in FY2025.

  • Margins: Gross margin is healthy at 50.3% (TTM). However, operating margin is only 11.7% (TTM), dragged down by high R&D ($8.09B, 23.4% of revenue) and SGA ($4.14B, 12.0% of revenue). Net profit margin is 13.4% (TTM).

  • Cash Flow: Operating cash flow is strong at $7.71B (FY2025). Free cash flow is $6.74B (FY2025), yielding a FCF yield of only 1.16% at the current market cap. CapEx is manageable at $974M (FY2025), representing 2.8% of revenue.

  • Balance Sheet: The balance sheet is solid. Total debt is $4.47B, but net debt is negative (-$1.07B), meaning cash ($5.54B) exceeds debt. The current ratio is 2.72x, and the quick ratio is 1.96x. However, goodwill and intangibles are massive at $41.8B (54% of total assets), a legacy of the Xilinx acquisition.

  • Key Concern: Stock-based compensation (SBC) is $1.64B (FY2025), which is 24% of operating cash flow. This dilutes shareholders significantly and is a hidden cost that depresses true free cash flow per share.

⚠️ Risks

  1. AI GPU Market Share Loss #critical

    2. AMD's MI300/MI400 series is competing against NVDA's dominant CUDA ecosystem. If AMD fails to gain meaningful share in the $100B+ data center GPU market, revenue growth will stall. The stock's valuation is entirely dependent on this narrative.

  2. Valuation Multiple Compression #critical

    3. At 147.6x P/E, the stock is priced for perfection. A 10% miss on guidance or a shift in investor sentiment away from AI hype could cause a 30-50% correction. The recent 8.1% single-day drop is a warning sign.

  3. Customer Concentration & Cyclicality #medium

    4. AMD's semi-custom business (game consoles) is tied to the console cycle, which is mature. A downturn in the PC market or enterprise IT spending would hurt the Computing and Graphics segment.

  4. Stock-Based Compensation Dilution #medium

    5. SBC of $1.64B (FY2025) represents 4.7% of revenue. This is a real cost that reduces economic earnings per share. If SBC continues to grow faster than revenue, it will depress intrinsic value.

  5. Geopolitical / Supply Chain #low

    6. AMD relies on TSMC for advanced chip manufacturing. Any disruption in Taiwan or export controls on AI chips to China could materially impact revenue.
