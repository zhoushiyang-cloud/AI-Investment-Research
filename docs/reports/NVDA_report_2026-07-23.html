> [!abstract] Executive Summary

  • Dominant AI Franchise: NVDA is the undisputed leader in accelerated computing, with its Data Center segment driving explosive growth. TTM revenue of $215.9B represents a 65% YoY increase from FY2025, fueled by insatiable demand for AI training and inference.
  • Exceptional Profitability: The company boasts a TTM net profit margin of 63.0% and a gross margin of 74.1%, reflecting immense pricing power and a superior product mix. ROIC stands at a staggering 63.0%.
  • Valuation is Elevated but Justified: At ~32.3x TTM P/E and ~26.6x EV/EBITDA, the stock trades at a premium to historical averages and peers. However, this is supported by a PEG ratio of 0.30, indicating it is cheap relative to its near-term earnings growth rate.
  • Key Risk is Demand Normalization: The primary risk is a cyclical downturn or a shift in customer spending patterns (e.g., from training to inference) that could compress margins and slow growth. The stock's high beta of 2.21 amplifies downside risk in a market correction.

> [!info] Business Overview

  • Core Business: NVDA designs graphics processing units (GPUs) and accelerated computing platforms. Its core moat is the CUDA software ecosystem, which locks developers into its hardware for AI, HPC, and visualization workloads.
  • Key Segments:
  • Compute & Networking (Data Center): The primary growth engine, encompassing AI/HPC platforms, networking (Mellanox), and enterprise AI software. This segment accounts for an estimated ~85-90% of revenue based on the TTM revenue of $215.9B and the scale of the Data Center business.
  • Graphics (Gaming & Pro Vis): Includes GeForce GPUs for gaming, Quadro/RTX for professional visualization, and the Omniverse platform. This segment is a significant cash generator but is growing at a slower pace than Data Center.
  • Competitive Position: NVDA holds a near-monopoly in the AI training GPU market. Its primary competitors are AMD and INTC in discrete GPUs, and AVGO in custom AI chips (ASICs). NVDA's key advantage is its full-stack offering (hardware + CUDA + networking), which competitors have yet to match effectively.
  • Strategic Partnerships: The company has deep partnerships with all major cloud providers (AMZN, GOOGL, MSFT) and enterprise IT vendors. The filing notes a notable partnership with Kroger Co., highlighting expansion into retail AI use cases.

> [!example] Financial Analysis

  • Revenue Growth: TTM revenue is $215.9B, up from $130.5B in FY2025 and $60.9B in FY2024. This represents a 65.4% YoY growth rate, decelerating from the prior year's 114% but still at an extraordinary pace.
  • Profitability: TTM gross margin is 74.1%, operating margin is 64.0%, and net margin is 63.0%. This is best-in-class among large-cap tech. The company generated $102.7B in operating cash flow and $96.7B in free cash flow (FCF yield of ~2.3%).
  • Balance Sheet: The company is fortress-like. It has $62.6B in cash & short-term investments against $11.4B in total debt (net cash position of ~$51.2B). The current ratio is a healthy 3.44.
  • Capital Allocation: NVDA is aggressively returning capital to shareholders. In FY2026, it repurchased $40.1B in stock and paid $974M in dividends. SBC is modest at 2.7% of revenue, which is well-controlled for a tech company of this scale.

> [!warning] Risks

  1. Demand Normalization / Cyclical Downturn #critical
  • The semiconductor industry is cyclical. A slowdown in enterprise AI spending or a shift in customer preference to custom ASICs (e.g., from AVGO or MRVL) could lead to a sharp deceleration in revenue growth. The stock's high beta (2.21) would amplify any earnings miss.
  1. Geopolitical / Export Controls #critical
  • Escalating US-China trade tensions and export restrictions on advanced AI chips (e.g., to China) could materially reduce a significant addressable market. The company's long description notes operations in Taiwan and China, adding supply chain risk.
  1. Competition from Custom Silicon (ASICs) #medium
  • Major cloud customers (AMZN, GOOGL, MSFT) are developing their own AI chips. While NVDA's software moat is strong, a significant shift toward in-house ASICs for inference workloads could erode market share and pricing power over the next 2-3 years.
  1. Valuation Compression #medium
  • At 32.3x TTM earnings, the stock is priced for perfection. Any disappointment in growth rates, even if still positive, could lead to a multiple contraction, causing the stock to fall even if earnings rise.

> [!tip] Catalysts

  1. Blackwell Architecture Ramp #near-term
  • The next-generation GPU platform is expected to drive a significant product cycle. Successful launch and adoption by hyperscalers could lead to upward revisions in revenue estimates for the next 2-3 quarters.
  1. Enterprise AI Adoption #medium-term
  • Beyond cloud providers, the deployment of AI in enterprise IT (e.g., the Kroger partnership) represents a massive untapped market. Growth in this segment could sustain the company's high growth rates for years.
  1. Software & Recurring Revenue Expansion #medium-term
  • The NVIDIA AI Enterprise software suite is a high-margin, recurring revenue stream. As adoption scales, it could improve the quality of earnings and justify a higher multiple, similar to MSFT or ORCL.
