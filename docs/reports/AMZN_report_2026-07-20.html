📋 Executive Summary
- Amazon delivered a standout FY2025, with total revenue of $716.9B (+12.4% YoY) and operating income surging to $80.0B (+16.6% YoY), driven by margin expansion in AWS and retail.
- Operating margins hit a record 11.2% (vs 10.8% in FY2024), fueled by cost optimization, higher-margin advertising revenue, and AWS profitability.
- Free cash flow (FCF) generation is robust: Operating cash flow of $139.5B was partially offset by $131.8B in capex, yielding FCF of ~$7.7B. However, the massive capex spend signals aggressive investment in AI infrastructure.

ℹ️ Business Overview
Amazon is a global technology conglomerate operating across three core segments:
- Amazon Web Services (AWS): Cloud computing and AI infrastructure. Estimated ~16% of total revenue but contributes >60% of operating profit.
- North America Retail: E-commerce, advertising, and Prime subscriptions. ~60% of revenue.
- International Retail: Higher-growth but lower-margin international e-commerce. ~24% of revenue.

>

Competitive Position: Amazon holds a dominant ~40% share of global cloud infrastructure (vs MSFT Azure ~24%, GOOGL GCP ~11%). In e-commerce, it commands ~38% of US online retail. The company’s competitive moat is built on logistics scale, AWS’s switching costs, and a massive data/ML flywheel. Key growth vectors include advertising (now a $56B+ business), AI/ML services (Bedrock, Trainium), and logistics-as-a-service.

📊 Financial Analysis
Revenue & Growth: FY2025 revenue of $716.9B grew 12.4% YoY from $637.9B in FY2024. This was driven by AWS acceleration (likely +19% YoY) and robust advertising growth (+24% YoY). Retail margins improved as lower cost-to-serve (regionalization) took hold.

>

Margins: Gross profit of $153.6B (21.4% margin) improved 160bps YoY. The real story is operating income: $80.0B (11.2% margin) vs $68.6B (10.8%) in FY2024. SGA expense declined to 1.6% of revenue (from 1.8%), reflecting efficiency gains. Depreciation & amortization surged to $65.8B (+24.6% YoY), a direct result of heavy AI capex.

>

Cash Flow & Balance Sheet: Operating cash flow of $139.5B (+20.4% YoY) was strong. However, capex exploded to $131.8B (vs $83.0B in FY2024), a 59% increase, primarily for data centers and AI chips. This suppressed levered FCF. The balance sheet is pristine: $123.0B in cash/short-term investments vs $65.6B in long-term debt. Total assets grew to $818.0B.

>

Key Metric: Net income of $77.7B (EPS $7.17 diluted) was up 31% YoY, driven by operating leverage and a lower effective tax rate (19.7% vs 13.5% in FY2024).

ℹ️ Valuation Assessment
- Current Multiples: At ~$220/share, AMZN trades at ~30.7x trailing P/E (on $7.17 EPS) and ~20x EV/EBITDA (EV ~$2.3T, EBITDA ~$115B). This is a premium to the S&P 500 (22x P/E) but in line with mega-cap tech peers like MSFT (32x) and GOOGL (23x).
- Historical Context: AMZN’s 5-year average P/E is ~55x, so the current multiple is compressed. However, this compression is due to margin normalization (earnings growing faster than revenue). On a P/S basis, AMZN trades at ~3.2x, below its 5-year average of ~3.8x.
- Justification: The premium is warranted given (1) AWS’s accelerating growth from AI, (2) expanding retail margins, and (3) a $2T+ TAM in cloud. The market is pricing in ~15% EPS CAGR over the next 3 years, which is achievable if AI capex yields returns.

⚠️ Risks
1. AI Capex Overbuild #critical
Capex surged to $131.8B in FY2025 (+59% YoY). If AI workload demand decelerates or competitors (MSFT/GOOGL) commoditize AI services, Amazon could face significant asset impairments and margin compression. Depreciation alone is now $65.8B and growing.
2. Regulatory & Antitrust Pressure #medium
The FTC lawsuit (filed 2023) and potential EU Digital Markets Act enforcement could force changes to Amazon’s marketplace practices, impacting third-party seller fees and advertising revenue (~$56B). A breakup scenario, while low probability, would destroy the flywheel.
3. Retail Margin Reversion #medium
Retail operating margins improved to ~6.5% in North America, but this is partially due to lapping tough comps and one-time cost cuts. Rising wage pressures (warehouse labor) and fuel costs could compress margins back toward 4-5%.
4. AWS Growth Deceleration #low
AWS growth is currently driven by AI inference workloads. If enterprises optimize cloud spend or shift to on-premise AI solutions, AWS growth could slow from ~19% to <12%, compressing the valuation multiple.

💡 Catalysts
1. AWS AI Revenue Inflection #near-term
Amazon’s custom Trainium2 chips and Bedrock AI platform are gaining traction. Expect AWS to report accelerating revenue growth (20%+ in Q1 2026) as AI workloads move from pilot to production. This would drive a re-rating.
2. Advertising Margin Expansion #near-term
Advertising revenue (now >$56B) carries 50%+ gross margins. As Amazon expands Prime Video ads and sponsored brands, incremental ad dollars drop directly to operating income. A 200bps increase in ad mix could add $14B to operating profit.
3. Free Cash Flow Inflection #medium-term
As AI capex peaks (expected in H2 2026), FCF could surge to $60B+ by FY2027. The market will reward this with multiple expansion, similar to META’s 2023 re-rating.
4. Logistics-as-a-Service (Buy with Prime) #medium-term
Amazon is monetizing its logistics network by offering fulfillment to third-party merchants. This could become a $10B+ revenue stream with high incremental margins, further diversifying earnings.

💬 Recommendation
BUY — High Conviction
12-Month Price Target: $280 (upside of ~27% from current ~$220)
Thesis: Amazon is in a unique position where its two largest profit drivers (AWS AI and advertising) are both accelerating simultaneously. The record capex spend is a feature, not a bug—it builds an unassailable lead in AI infrastructure. While near-term FCF is suppressed, the earnings power of the business is underappreciated. We see FY2026 EPS of $8.50+ and an eventual re-rating to 35x P/E as AI monetization becomes visible. Key risk is execution on AI ROI, but management’s track record (AWS, FBA) supports confidence.
